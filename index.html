              Calculation table

              Every term of the material balance at each pressure, in field units. Untick a row to leave it out of the fit.

              Method

              Havlena and Odeh (1963) wrote the material balance as a straight line. With both N and m unknown, a plot of y against x gives N as the intercept and mN as the slope. Both axes hold (1 + m) Efw, so the line is fitted again with the new m until m stops changing, which takes a few rounds. Without Efw the plot is F/Eo against Eg/Eo. For a combination drive, the water influx We is taken off F first; for a pot aquifer, K is changed by trial and error until the points make a straight line, and the dashboard can also find the K that fits best by least squares.

              Underground withdrawal
              F=Np [Bo+(Rp−Rs) Bg]+Wp Bw
              Oil and dissolved gas
              Eo=(Bo−Boi)+(Rsi−Rs) Bg
              Gas-cap expansion
              Eg=Boi (BgBgi−1)
              Connate water and rock
              Efw=Boi Cw Swc+Cf1−Swc Δp
              Material balance
              F=N [Eo+m Eg+(1+m) Efw]+We
              Straight line
              F−WeEo+(1+m) Efw=N+mN EgEo+(1+m) Efw
              Gas cap from the line
              m=slopeinterceptG=mN BoiBgi
              Drive indices
              DDI=N EoFSDI=N m EgFCDI=N (1+m) EfwFWDI=WeF
              Pot aquifer
              We=K (pi−p),K=(cw+cf) Wi f

              Units used in the calculation: p in psia, Np in STB, Gp in SCF, Rs and Rp in SCF/STB, Bo in rb/STB, Bg in rb/SCF (ft³/SCF ÷ 5.615), Cw and Cf in psi⁻¹. Values in other units are converted first. The 95 % ranges come from the least-squares fit; the range of m uses the delta method.
